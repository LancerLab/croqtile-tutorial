\subsection*{Summary of New Syntax}

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Syntax} & \textbf{Meaning} \\
\midrule
\texttt{parallel p by N} & N-way parallelism, index \texttt{p} runs 0..N-1 concurrently \\
\texttt{parallel \{px, py\} by [M, N]} & Multi-dimensional parallel level \textless{}br\textgreater{} (\texttt{p} and \texttt{q} correspond to one parallel level) \\
\texttt{parallel p by A, q by B} & Comma-separated parallel levels \textless{}br\textgreater{} (\texttt{p} and \texttt{q} correspond to different parallel levels) \\
\texttt{parallel p by N :\allowbreak{} block} & Map to blocks \\
\texttt{parallel q by N :\allowbreak{} thread} & Map to threads within a block \\
\texttt{parallel w by N :\allowbreak{} group} & Map to groups \\
\texttt{=\textgreater{} shared} & DMA destination: block-scoped shared storage \\
\texttt{foreach index = \{m, n, k\} in [a, b, c]} & Named tuple destructuring in a \texttt{foreach} \\
\texttt{lhs.span(0)} & Extract one dimension of a tensor's shape \\
\texttt{p \# m} & Compose outer tile index \texttt{p} with inner offset \texttt{m} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The tiled DMA matmul you built in this part of the guide is the structural backbone of every high-performance kernel. Section~\ref{sec:3-7} replaces the scalar \texttt{.at()} arithmetic in the inner loop with \textbf{\texttt{mma}} operations --- a matrix contraction that consumes a small tile per step instead of one element at a time.
