Of course, putting those decisions in the language does not make writing a kernel trivial; it moves the difficulty into the compiler, where it can be discharged once instead of rediscovered by every program. The compile-time safety checks have to decide shapes and indices before anything runs. Symbolic dimensions have to survive until the program runs, so one source serves many input sizes. The abstractions have to stay zero-cost, and the constructs have to stay few enough that a person can hold all of them in mind. Those constraints shape almost every rule in the following chapters; giving you a sense of how the compiler meets them is one of the purposes of this guide.
